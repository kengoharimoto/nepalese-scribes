Sources: NGMCP catalogue entries (Nepal-German Manuscript Cataloguing Project), and Cecil Bendall, Catalogue of the Buddhist Sanskrit Manuscripts in the University Library, Cambridge (1883), whose manuscripts appear as “Cambridge Add. …” (parts of composite numbers as “Add. 1691 (II)”). For Bendall’s manuscripts the date is recalculated from the colophon (era, year, month, pakṣa, tithi, weekday, nakṣatra) with the Pañcāṅga program of M. Yano and M. Fushimi, not taken from Bendall’s A.D. conversions, made when the Nepalese calendar was little understood: where the colophon states a weekday the day is verified (the standard reading first, then a current year, a Kārttikādi or pūrṇimānta month, a tithi current after sunrise), and the reading used is named with the date. Bendall’s Harṣa-era date (Add. 1049) is read in the Aṃśuvarman (“Mānadeva”) saṃvat, which K. P. Malla (CNAS Journal 32.1, 2005) showed to be the Kārttikādi Śaka with 500 dropped: CE = year + 576 from Kārttika to December, + 577 from January (it fits the only two Licchavi-period dates with a weekday, Aṃśuvarman’s repoussé at Cāṅgu, 4 Feb 608, and the Suśrutasaṃhitā, C 80/7, 13 Apr 878). Bendall’s A.D. is kept, and marked, only where the colophon gives no era. Persons in Bendall’s manuscripts come from a reading of his whole description, English notes and quoted colophons together. Persons come from two places: the NGMCP catalogue fields Scribe, Donor and King, and a reading of each quoted colophon by Gemini Flash, with a second reading by Claude Opus wherever the first was uncertain or disagreed with the catalogue. Each attestation is marked with its source: cat catalogue field, flash Gemini reading, opus checked by Opus.

Dates are recalculated from the colophon with the Pañcāṅga program of M. Yano and M. Fushimi (Kathmandu sunrise): era and year from the catalogue and the colophon reading, month, pakṣa, tithi, weekday and nakṣatra from the colophon reading (or the catalogue’s date field). Where a weekday is stated the day is checked: “verified” means the standard reading fits (NS with amānta months; VS and Śaka with pūrṇimānta months for the dark fortnight); “verified with one departure” means a tithi current later that day or expired, or the other month system, fits. Tested against the same dates with deliberately wrong weekdays, about 1 in 20 “verified” and 1 in 5 “verified with one departure” dates would fit by chance. Readings that fit wrong dates about as often as right ones (a current year, year + 1, Kārttikādi) are only mentioned. Lakṣmaṇasena dates agree with their weekdays only at chance level under any fixed epoch, so they are converted (LS + 1119) but not verified. Without a month, the year is converted as before (NS + 880, VS − 57, ŚS + 78), ± 1. A paper manuscript dated before 1300 is treated as undated (the date is given as doubtful): such dates are abbreviated or misread years, or the exemplar’s date copied with its colophon. Two of the oldest dated manuscripts, the Skandapurāṇa (B 11/4) and the Suśrutasaṃhitā (C 80/7), have no catalogue entry and come from the NGMCP title list.

Licchavi inscriptions (5th–9th century) come from the e-text edition with its concordance to Gnoli (Gn.), D. Vajracarya (DV), HJ and Regmi (R.), labelled “Licchavi inscr. N” by the edition’s number. Each was read by Gemini Flash (Opus where uncertain) for the persons it names: kings, dūtakas, officials, donors, recipients, composers and engravers (“other” covers dūtakas and officials); ancestors named only in genealogies are left out, since the inscription’s date is not theirs, and Licchavi persons are not grouped with the persons of manuscripts. Their dates are calculated afresh from the year, month, pakṣa and tithi of the inscription, not taken from the editors: following K. P. Malla (2005), both the earlier saṃvat (386–535) and Aṃśuvarman’s (from 29) are the Kārttikādi current Śaka, the latter with 500 dropped (CE = year + 76/77 and + 576/577). Only Aṃśuvarman’s gold repoussé at Cāṅgu (inscr. 80) names a weekday; it verifies as Sunday 4 Feb 608. The others are computed, not verified.

Undated manuscripts get an estimated date (hollow dots and circles) from the people and places they name: a king attested in at least two dated manuscripts (the king's attested span ± 5 years), two persons named together in a dated manuscript (± 25), a person's dated span (± 20; graded by whether a title or residence also matches), or a specific locality whose dated manuscripts fall within 150 years. The evidence is listed with each estimate.

Persons are grouped by normalized name (without śrī and without titles such as śarman, vajrācārya, upādhyāya). A name whose dated manuscripts are more than 60 years apart is split into separate persons, so homonyms are separated only by date.
